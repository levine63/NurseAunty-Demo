Painted ORS set for Rwanda, mixing pictures redrawn with a jug - how the three pictures were asked for

Made 2026-11-12 with Google's Gemini picture model (gemini-nano-banana-2.1), by the same method as
the first set (rw_ors_painted_set_v1.txt, ADR-075): the people and the things they touch were drawn
on a flat magenta background, cut out, and laid over the one stored picture of the room.

Why they were redrawn: the first set's pictures for adding the sachet, mixing and covering show a
plain plastic water bottle with no one-litre line. Under the measuring sentence for a half-litre
bottle ("Fill it 2 times. Pour each fill into a clean mixing container.") a sachet going into a
water bottle can be read as "put the sachet in this bottle", which is ORS at twice the strength
(ADR-080). David, 2026-11-12, asked which container the Rwandan mixing pictures should show:
"Mixing container option A", a container nobody could take for a small bottle (ADR-081).

The three bottle pictures they replace (06 pour sachet, 07 shake, 13 cover it) left the app's
picture folder the same day; they are in the repository's history and on David's laptop.

The app keeps the new ones as WebP files in apps/pwa-sandbox/assets/generated/:
  generated_rw_ors_06_pour_sachet_v2.webp   (request 06_pour_sachet_jug1l)
  generated_rw_ors_07_stir_v1.webp          (request 07_stir_jug1l)
  generated_rw_ors_13_cover_it_v2.webp      (request 13_cover_jug1l_b)

Two rounds, nine requests
-------------------------
Round one (four requests: a sheet of a big jug, then the three scenes) is NOT in the app. It asked
for "a big, wide, clear plastic jug ... as tall as the mother's forearm ... filled up to its one
bold dark line". The independent review measured what came back: a jug of two to three litres,
about two-thirds full, with no line on it, and drawn at about twice the volume in the first
picture as in the second. A caregiver who copied it would make ORS that is too weak. Lesson: say
the size against something in the picture, and do not ask for "big".

Round two (four requests: a sheet of a one-litre jug beside the cup, then the three scenes) asked
for a small one-litre jug, one and a half times as tall as the metal cup, nearly full, with a
thick black line painted round it at the water's surface, and the cup beside it in every scene so
that its size is fixed. Its first drawing of the covering scene let the background colour show
through the top of the clear jug, left out the line and drew a darker table, so that scene was
asked for once more with those three things said (the ninth request).

Every request was answered at the first attempt. What they cost was not recorded; they were
charged to David's own key. The script, every picture of both rounds and the logs are on David's
laptop in Claude/nurseaunty-consistency-trial (ors_jug.py and the folder ors_jug).

They are prototype drafts. Rwandan reviewers check each one (ADR-077): does it look like Rwanda,
and does it show the right action and amount. Known already: the jug's size is fixed only against
the cup beside it, and measured on the three pictures it is 1.55 to 1.75 times the cup's height
and about one and a half times its width, so the cup is about a third of the jug: that fits a mug
and not the five small tea cups of the measuring picture; the covering picture shows an empty cup
and a spoon beside the covered jug and nothing being thrown away; a clear plastic measuring jug
may not be what a Rwandan household has; and pictures 03,
04, 05, 08, 17 and 23 of the first set still show the bottle. None of those six is shown in an
app, and they would need the jug before they are.
